Overview

Why Reach-In Refrigerator Not Cooling Happens

Reach-In Refrigerator Not Cooling can look alarming, but it is often repairable. The likely culprits are dirty condenser, fan failure and gasket failure, and each has distinct signs. Below you will find what to look for, what you can safely check and what a technician tests.

Food safety: if product has been above 41°F for an extended period, move perishable items to working refrigeration and follow your local health department guidance.

Symptoms

Symptoms You May Notice

  • Interior above 41°F
  • Compressor running constantly
  • Fans not running
  • Frost on evaporator
  • Water leaking
Try First

Quick Checks Before You Call

  • Check that the condenser is not caked with dust
  • Make sure vents are not blocked by product
  • Check the gasket
  • Verify the thermostat setting

These checks are meant to be safe for most people. If anything involves gas, electrical panels or refrigerant, stop and call a technician.

Diagnosis

Most Common Causes

Dirty condenser

Reach-ins near cooking lines clog quickly.

Fan failure

Airflow stops.

Gasket failure

Cold air escapes.

Electrical issues

Start parts fail.

Defrost issues

Ice blocks airflow.

Professional Diagnosis

What a Technician Checks

  • Measure interior temperatures
  • Clean and inspect condenser
  • Test fans and electricals
  • Inspect gaskets and defrost

Because reach-ins sit next to hot cooking equipment, they work in difficult conditions. Dirty condensers, tired gaskets and stressed fans are the most common failures we see.

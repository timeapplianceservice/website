Overview

Why Refrigerator Freezing Food Happens

Refrigerator Freezing Food can look alarming, but it is often repairable. The likely culprits are thermostat or sensor faults, damper problems and blocked vents, and each has distinct signs. Below you will find what to look for, what you can safely check and what a technician tests.

Symptoms

Symptoms You May Notice

  • Food freezing in the fridge
  • Ice on lettuce
  • Frozen drinks
  • Temperature too low
  • Frost near vents
Try First

Quick Checks Before You Call

  • Check the temperature setting
  • Make sure food is not touching the vents
  • Check the thermostat or sensor
  • Check the damper

These checks are meant to be safe for most people. If anything involves gas, electrical panels or refrigerant, stop and call a technician.

Diagnosis

Most Common Causes

Thermostat or sensor faults

The fridge overcools.

Damper problems

Too much cold air enters the fridge.

Blocked vents

Cold air is trapped near food.

Control board issues

Cooling is not regulated.

Setting too cold

Dial or setting is too low.

Professional Diagnosis

What a Technician Checks

  • Check temperature settings
  • Test thermostat or sensor
  • Check damper
  • Test the control board

Most refrigerator problems come down to a handful of parts: fans, defrost components, sensors, door seals and control boards. We check them in a logical order and explain what we find.

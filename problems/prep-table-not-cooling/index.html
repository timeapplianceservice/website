Overview

Why Prep Table Not Cooling Happens

Prep Table Not Cooling is one of the most common service calls we handle. In most cases it comes down to a small number of causes, such as blocked airflow, fan failure or lid or gasket leaks. A proper diagnosis finds the real cause before you pay for a repair.

Food safety: if product has been above 41°F for an extended period, move perishable items to working refrigeration and follow your local health department guidance.

Symptoms

Symptoms You May Notice

  • Rail warm
  • Pans above 41°F
  • Cabinet cold but rail warm
  • Compressor constantly running
  • Ice on the rail
Try First

Quick Checks Before You Call

  • Check pans are not overfilled
  • Make sure lid is closed when not in use
  • Check that vents are clear
  • Clean the condenser if dusty

These checks are meant to be safe for most people. If anything involves gas, electrical panels or refrigerant, stop and call a technician.

Diagnosis

Most Common Causes

Blocked airflow

Overfilled pans stop cold air circulation.

Fan failure

Air does not move to the rail.

Lid or gasket leaks

Warm air enters.

Thermostat issues

Wrong temperature control.

Condenser or compressor problems

The sealed system lacks capacity.

Professional Diagnosis

What a Technician Checks

  • Measure rail and cabinet temperatures
  • Check fans and airflow
  • Inspect lid and gaskets
  • Test sealed system performance

A prep table has two cooling zones to maintain, the cabinet and the top rail. We test airflow, thermostat control and the sealed system to find why one or both are warm.

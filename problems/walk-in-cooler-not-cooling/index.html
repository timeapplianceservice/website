Overview

Why Walk-In Cooler Not Cooling Happens

When you are dealing with walk-in cooler not cooling, the cause is rarely the whole walk-in cooler. It is usually one failed part, most often iced evaporator, failed condensing unit or dirty condenser coil. Testing in the right order is what separates a quick fix from a repeat visit.

Food safety: if product has been above 41°F for an extended period, move perishable items to working refrigeration and follow your local health department guidance.

Symptoms

Symptoms You May Notice

  • Box reading above 41°F
  • Condensing unit running without cooling
  • Warm product at the back of the box
  • Evaporator fans not running
  • Alarm sounding for high temperature
Try First

Quick Checks Before You Call

  • Confirm the door is fully closed and nothing blocks the evaporator fans
  • Check that the condensing unit has power and the disconnect is on
  • Look for ice on the evaporator coil
  • Check temperatures with a probe thermometer and move perishables if the box stays warm

These checks are meant to be safe for most people. If anything involves gas, electrical panels or refrigerant, stop and call a technician.

Diagnosis

Most Common Causes

Iced evaporator

Ice blocks airflow through the coil and the box cannot cool.

Failed condensing unit

Compressor, contactor, capacitor or fan failures stop the refrigeration cycle.

Dirty condenser coil

Grease and dust raise pressure and cut capacity.

Evaporator fan failure

Without airflow the cold stays at the coil.

Refrigerant loss

A leak lowers capacity until the box drifts warm.

Professional Diagnosis

What a Technician Checks

  • Measure box and coil temperatures
  • Check evaporator and condenser fans
  • Test compressor amps, capacitor and contactor
  • Evaluate defrost operation and refrigerant pressures

Walk-in coolers fail in predictable ways: iced evaporators, dirty condensers, failed fans, tired door gaskets and weak electrical parts. Our technicians check each in turn and repair what is actually broken.

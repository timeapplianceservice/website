Overview

Why Furnace Not Heating Happens

Furnace Not Heating can look alarming, but it is often repairable. The likely culprits are dirty flame sensor, failed igniter and clogged filter, and each has distinct signs. Below you will find what to look for, what you can safely check and what a technician tests.

Symptoms

Symptoms You May Notice

  • Furnace blowing cold air
  • No heat
  • Burner lights then shuts off
  • Blower runs but no warmth
  • Error code flashing
Try First

Quick Checks Before You Call

  • Set the thermostat to heat and raise the setting
  • Check the filter
  • Make sure the gas valve and breaker are on
  • If you smell gas, leave and call your gas utility

These checks are meant to be safe for most people. If anything involves gas, electrical panels or refrigerant, stop and call a technician.

Diagnosis

Most Common Causes

Dirty flame sensor

The burner shuts off when flame is not proven.

Failed igniter

The burner does not light.

Clogged filter

Overheating trips the limit switch.

Inducer or pressure switch faults

Safety circuits prevent ignition.

Thermostat or control faults

The heat call does not reach the furnace.

Professional Diagnosis

What a Technician Checks

  • Observe the start-up sequence
  • Test igniter and flame sensor
  • Check airflow and limits
  • Check thermostat and board

Furnaces rely on a chain of parts, including the thermostat, inducer, igniter, flame sensor and blower. We follow that chain until we find the break and repair it.

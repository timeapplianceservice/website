Choosing a repair company can feel like a gamble, especially when an appliance has just failed and you need it fixed fast. A few simple checks help you separate dependable companies from the rest.

In this guide

  • Look for clear, upfront pricing
  • Check the warranty
  • Read real reviews
  • Ask about your brand and appliance type
  • Questions worth asking before you book
  • Red flags

Look for clear, upfront pricing

A good company explains its service call fee, what it covers and how repairs are priced before you book. Be cautious of very low advertised prices that grow once the technician arrives.

Ask whether the service call is credited or waived if you approve the repair. With us, the $135 service call is free when you go ahead with the repair.

Check the warranty

Ask what the repair warranty covers and for how long. Our repairs include a 30-day labor warranty and a 90-day parts warranty. A company that stands behind its work will say so clearly.

Read real reviews

Look at reviews on Google and read what customers describe: whether the technician explained the problem, arrived on time, quoted honestly and fixed it right. Patterns matter more than a single review. You can read our reviews on our Google profile.

Ask about your brand and appliance type

Some brands, especially premium built-in appliances, need specific parts and procedures. Ask whether the company works on your brand and what experience it has with it. Our technicians service premium brands such as Sub-Zero, Wolf, Thermador and Miele as well as everyday brands.

Questions worth asking before you book

  • What is the service call fee and what does it include?
  • Is the fee waived if I approve the repair?
  • Do you carry common parts on the truck?
  • What warranty do you provide on parts and labor?
  • Will you explain the diagnosis and quote before starting work?
  • Do you service my area and brand?

Red flags

  • Pressure to approve a repair immediately without a clear explanation.
  • Vague pricing or refusing to quote before work starts.
  • No warranty or unclear terms.
  • Reviews that all look similar or that never mention the actual repair.

When an appliance fails, the first question is usually whether to fix it or buy a new one. The answer depends on the age of the appliance, what failed and what the repair costs compared with a comparable unit. This guide gives you a simple way to think it through.

  • Start with the diagnosis

  • When replacement may make more sense

Start with the diagnosis

Never decide based on a symptom alone. A refrigerator that is not cooling might need a fan that costs little to fix, or it might need a sealed-system repair that costs far more. A professional diagnosis tells you which one you have, and that is what makes the decision possible.

Our $135 service call covers the diagnosis, and if you approve the repair the service call is free.

A simple rule of thumb

Many people use the 50 percent rule: if the repair costs more than half the price of a comparable new appliance, replacement deserves serious consideration. Age matters too. A repair on a three-year-old premium appliance is an easy call, while the same repair on a fifteen-year-old budget model is harder.

Think about the expected life of the appliance type. Refrigerators and ranges often last well over a decade, washers and dishwashers somewhat less. These are averages, and well-maintained appliances can exceed them.

Repairs that are usually worth it

  • Fans, sensors, thermostats and defrost parts on refrigerators and freezers.
  • Drain pumps, belts, door locks and inlet valves on washers.
  • Heating elements, igniters, belts and thermal fuses on dryers.
  • Igniters, elements, sensors and control boards on ovens and ranges.
  • Pumps, spray arms and latches on dishwashers.

When replacement may make more sense

  • The repair cost is close to the price of a comparable appliance.
  • The appliance has needed several repairs recently.
  • The failure involves a major component and the appliance is old.
  • Parts are discontinued or hard to find.
  • Safety concerns exist, such as repeated overheating.

Other things to weigh

Consider energy use, features you actually want, available space and whether the appliance is built into cabinetry. A built-in refrigerator or a pro-style range can be costly and disruptive to replace, which often tips the decision toward repair.

Also think about the warranty. Our repairs include a 30-day labor warranty and a 90-day parts warranty, which means you are protected after the repair.

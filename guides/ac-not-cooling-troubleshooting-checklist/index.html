A warm house on a hot day is stressful, and the cause is often something simple you can check in a few minutes. This checklist walks through the quick checks first, then shows the signs that mean it is time for a technician.

In this guide

  • Start with the thermostat
  • Check the air filter
  • Check power and the outdoor unit
  • Look for ice or water
  • Check airflow in the house
  • When to stop and call a technician

Start with the thermostat

Make sure the thermostat is set to cool, the temperature is set below the current room temperature and the fan is set to auto. If the display is blank, check the batteries. Smart thermostats can lose Wi-Fi or settings, so confirm the schedule is not holding the temperature higher than you expect.

Check the air filter

A dirty filter is the most common reason for poor cooling. It restricts airflow, which reduces cooling and can cause the indoor coil to ice over. Change the filter if it is dirty, and make a habit of checking it monthly during heavy use.

Check power and the outdoor unit

  • Look at the breaker panel for a tripped AC breaker and reset it once.
  • Check the outdoor disconnect box near the unit.
  • With the system calling for cooling, see whether the outdoor fan is turning and the unit is humming normally.
  • If the outdoor unit hums but the fan does not spin, switch the system off and call us. That pattern often points to a weak capacitor.
  • Keep plants, leaves and debris at least a couple of feet away from the outdoor unit.

Look for ice or water

Ice on the indoor coil or refrigerant line means airflow or refrigerant is restricted. Turn the system off and let the ice thaw with the fan on. Running a frozen system can damage it. Water around the indoor unit usually means a clogged condensate drain, which can also shut the system off through a safety float switch.

Check airflow in the house

Open vents and returns, and make sure furniture or rugs are not blocking them. Weak airflow from every vent points to the blower, filter or coil. Weak airflow from only one room is a distribution issue.

When to stop and call a technician

Our $135 service call covers the diagnosis, and it is free if you approve the repair.

  • The outdoor unit hums but does not start.
  • The system trips the breaker repeatedly.
  • You see ice that returns after thawing.
  • You hear grinding, squealing or banging.
  • You smell burning.
  • You have done the simple checks and the house still will not cool.

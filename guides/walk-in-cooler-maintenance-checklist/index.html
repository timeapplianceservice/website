A walk-in cooler is one of the most important and most overlooked pieces of equipment in a kitchen. Most failures start small, with a dirty coil, a tired gasket or a fan that is slowing down. This checklist shows what to look at and how often, so you catch trouble before product is at risk.

In this guide

  • Daily checks anyone on staff can do
  • Weekly and monthly tasks
  • Seasonal and professional maintenance
  • Warning signs that need a call
  • Common mistakes to avoid

Daily checks anyone on staff can do

  • Read the box temperature and compare it to your target. Many operators aim for 36 to 38°F so there is margin below 41°F.
  • Make sure the door closes fully and latches, and that nothing is propping it open.
  • Listen for the evaporator fans and look for visible ice on the coil or ceiling.
  • Look for water on the floor and any new noises from the condensing unit.
  • Keep product off the floor and away from the evaporator so air can circulate.

Weekly and monthly tasks

Wipe door gaskets and check for tears, flat spots or gaps. A gasket that no longer seals lets in humid air that frosts the coil and forces the system to run constantly.

Check the strip curtain, door sweep and hinges. Look at the condenser coil, and if it is dusty or greasy, schedule cleaning. A restricted condenser is one of the leading causes of warm walk-ins in summer.

Check the drain line for slime or ice and make sure the drain pan area is clear.

Seasonal and professional maintenance

A technician should clean the condenser and evaporator coils, test fan motors and bearings, inspect door heaters, test defrost heaters, timers and terminations, check electrical connections and amp draws, and review refrigerant pressures and operation.

Many operations schedule professional maintenance two to four times a year. Scheduling one visit before summer is the highest-value choice, because heat is when marginal equipment fails.

Warning signs that need a call

  • Box temperature drifting upward over several days.
  • Ice forming on the evaporator, fan guards or ceiling.
  • Condensing unit running nonstop or cycling on and off rapidly.
  • Water pooling inside or outside the box.
  • Loud humming, grinding or rattling from the condensing unit or fans.
  • Frost around the door frame that keeps returning.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not chip ice off the evaporator with tools, which can puncture the coil. Do not prop the door open during stocking for long periods. Do not overload shelves so that air cannot move. Do not ignore a slowly rising temperature, because it rarely fixes itself.

Keep a simple log with the date, time and temperature. The log supports your food safety program and gives the technician valuable history.

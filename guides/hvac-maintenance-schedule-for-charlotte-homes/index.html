The Charlotte area has long, humid summers and cool winters, which means your system works for most of the year. A simple seasonal schedule keeps it reliable and helps you catch problems early.

Spring: get ready for the cooling season

Spring is the best time for a professional AC tune-up, before the first heat wave brings every technician's schedule to a crunch. A visit covers electrical testing, coil and filter checks, a drain flush, motor inspection and temperature and airflow measurements.

At home, clear leaves and debris from around the outdoor unit and trim plants back. Change the filter and test the thermostat before you need it.

Summer: watch for early warning signs

During summer, check the filter monthly. Watch for longer run times, uneven cooling, water near the indoor unit or an outdoor unit that hums without starting. Catching a weak capacitor early is far easier than dealing with a full breakdown on the hottest day of the year.

Humidity matters in the Carolinas. A drain that clogs with algae can shut the system down, so a drain flush is worth including in your annual visit.

Fall: get ready for heating

Fall is the time to have the furnace or heat pump checked. A technician tests the ignition and flame sensing on gas furnaces, the blower and capacitor, safety switches and thermostat operation. For heat pumps, checks include defrost operation and the outdoor fan.

Change the filter before the first cold snap, and put fresh batteries in smoke and carbon monoxide detectors as part of your routine.

Winter: keep an eye on performance

Check the filter monthly, keep vents clear and note any short cycling or unusual noises. If your heat pump ices heavily or blows cool air for long periods, have it checked. Heat pumps do defrost briefly, but long or repeated cool air is a sign something needs attention.

A simple annual calendar

Many homeowners combine these into two visits a year, one in spring and one in fall.

  • March to April: AC tune-up and drain flush.
  • May to September: monthly filter checks and watch for warning signs.
  • October: heating check.
  • November to February: monthly filter checks and heat pump observation.
